What CalledIt is, in one line

CalledIt is a non-custodial product: when you sign up you get your own wallet, funds you deposit are delivered directly to that wallet, and we never hold your money. That shapes what data we do and do not touch.

Data we collect

  • Account identifiers. When you sign in through our authentication provider Privy, we receive the identifier you used (email address, phone number, or Apple/Google account identifier) and your user id.
  • Wallet address. The public blockchain address of the embedded wallet created for you. Blockchain addresses and their transactions are public by the nature of public blockchains.
  • Deposit metadata. When you fund your wallet through MoonPay, we receive transaction status events (identifiers, amounts, currency, status). We never receive or store your card number, bank details, or identity documents.
  • Technical data. Your IP address (used for security, including binding payment sessions to your device as required by our payment partner), browser and device basics, and server logs.

What we deliberately do not collect

Payment card data and know-your-customer identity documents are collected and processed by MoonPay directly under its own privacy policy, acting as an independent controller. We never see them.

Why we process your data

  • To provide the service (create your wallet, show your balance, generate deposit sessions) — performance of a contract, Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR.
  • Security and abuse prevention (IP binding of payment sessions, rate limiting, logs) — legitimate interest, Art. 6(1)(f).
  • Legal obligations (accounting, responding to lawful requests) — Art. 6(1)(c).

We do not sell personal data and we do not use it for third-party advertising.

Who receives data

  • Privy (authentication and embedded wallets)
  • MoonPay (fiat-to-crypto payments, independent controller)
  • Vercel (application hosting) and GitHub Pages (website hosting)

Some providers process data in the United States; transfers rely on the European Commission's Standard Contractual Clauses or an adequacy decision. Anything written to a public blockchain (your wallet address, balances, transactions) is public worldwide by design and cannot be deleted by anyone.

Retention

Account and transaction metadata are kept while your account exists and afterwards only as long as required for legal and accounting purposes. Server logs are kept briefly for security. On-chain data is permanent by nature.
